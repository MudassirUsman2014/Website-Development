My space web page

Outer space is the vast, near-perfect vacuum that exists beyond Earth's atmosphere and between celestial bodies. It begins approximately 62 miles (100 kilometers) above sea level at the Kármán line, where the air becomes too thin to sustain conventional flight or scatter light, causing the blue sky to turn black. Space is not entirely empty; it contains a low density of cosmic rays, plasma, magnetic fields, and interstellar dust.

Key Characteristics of Space

  • Extreme Temperatures: The baseline cosmic background temperature sits at 2.7 Kelvin (-270°C or -455°F).
  • Complete Silence: Sound waves require a physical medium like air to travel, making the vacuum of space completely quiet.
  • Mind-Boggling Scale: The observable universe spans a diameter of roughly 93 billion light-years.
  • Abundant Matter: It hosts billions of galaxies, each containing hundreds of billions of stars, planets, and planetary systems like our own.

Active Areas of Space Exploration

  • Crewed Missions: Astronauts regularly live aboard the International Space Station (ISS), with new crews continually arriving via spacecraft like the Soyuz MS-29 to monitor zero-gravity biology and spacecraft safety.
  • Lunar to Martian Pipelines: NASA's Artemis Program is actively stacking rockets to return humans to the Moon. This serves as a stepping stone for future crewed trips to Mars.

  • Deep Space Observations: Advanced instruments like the James Webb Space Telescope and newly deployed commercial satellites track stellar flares and map cosmic magnetic fields.
  • Interstellar Discoveries: Chemists continually identify organic building blocks, such as specific sugars (e.g., erythrulose), floating inside the gas clouds of the interstellar medium.
