What is the meaning weather?

Weather refers to the day-to-day state of the atmosphere at a specific time and place. It is defined by elements like temperature, humidity, wind, precipitation, and cloud cover, and changes rapidly over short periods.

Key Concepts

  • The Atmosphere: Almost all weather phenomena occur in the troposphere—the lowest layer of Earth's atmosphere.
  • Weather vs. Climate: While weather represents short-term atmospheric conditions, climate refers to the average weather patterns of a specific region measured over a long period, typically over 30 years.

Explore these 15 fascinating and scientific facts about weather, covering everything from the physics of raindrops to the sheer power of atmospheric storms:

  • Raindrops aren't teardrops: Contrary to popular belief, falling raindrops are shaped more like hamburger buns or jelly beans, flattened at the bottom by air pressure.
  • Lightning is hotter than the sun: A single bolt of lightning can reach temperatures up to 50,000° F (30,000° C), which is about five times hotter than the surface of the sun.
  • Virga is ghost rain: Sometimes precipitation falls from clouds but evaporates before ever reaching the ground, leaving streaks in the sky.
  • Clouds are incredibly heavy: A typical fluffy cumulus cloud that is just one cubic kilometer in size weighs about 1.1 million pounds (500,000 kg), or roughly 100 elephants.
  • Rain falls fast: An average large raindrop falls at speeds of about 22 mph (35 km/h)
  • Earth's coldest spot: The lowest natural temperature ever recorded on Earth was -128.6° F (-89.2° C) at Vostok Station in Antarctica on July 21, 1983.
  • The atmosphere has mass: The entire Earth's atmosphere weighs approximately 5.1 million billion tons.
  • Snow speed: Individual snowflakes usually fall quite slowly, at speeds between 1 and 5 miles per hour
  • Tornadoes can spin fire: Under rare and extreme conditions, intense heat from wildfires can create twisting columns of fire called fire whirls or "fire tornadoes".
