Main courses

Chinese
Authentic & Traditional Classics Mapo Tofu (麻婆豆腐) : A famous Sichuan dish made with silken tofu, minced pork or beef, and a fiery, mouth-numbing sauce.
Peking Duck (北京烤鸭): Beijing's signature roasted duck, famed for its incredibly crispy skin. It is typically served thinly sliced with sweet bean sauce, cucumber, and scallions wrapped in thin pancakes.
Red Braised Pork Belly (红烧肉): A melt-in-the-mouth Hunan/Jiangsu classic where pork belly is slow-braised in a sweet, savory mixture of soy sauce, sugar, ginger, and Shaoxing wine.
Kung Pao Chicken (宫保鸡丁): A stir-fry classic featuring diced chicken, peanuts, vegetables, and chili peppers, originating from the Sichuan province.
Dim Sum (点心): More of a dining style than a single dish, consisting of bite-sized portions of steamed or fried delicacies like Siu Mai (pork and shrimp dumplings) and Char Siu Bao (barbecue pork buns).
Street Food & Local Staples Jiaozi (饺子 - Chinese Dumplings): Classic folded dumplings filled with ground meat and vegetables, typically boiled, steamed, or pan-fried (potstickers).
Baozi (包子): Steamed, fluffy dough buns stuffed with savory fillings like pork, vegetables, or red bean paste.
Scallion Pancakes (葱油饼): Pan-fried, savory flatbreads folded with oil and minced scallions, crispy on the outside and chewy on the inside.
Biang Biang Noodles (陕西面食): Thick, incredibly wide hand-pulled noodles from Shaanxi province, usually topped with chili flakes, garlic, and hot oil.
Lanzhou Beef Noodle Soup (兰州牛肉拉面): A staple Chinese Muslim dish featuring hand-pulled wheat noodles, tender beef slices, and a clear, flavorful broth.
Savory Stir-Fries & Sides Tomato and Egg Stir-Fry (番茄炒蛋): A beloved, home-style dish made of soft-scrambled eggs and stir-fried tomatoes.
Twice-Cooked Pork (回锅肉): A signature Sichuan dish where pork belly is first boiled, then sliced and stir-fried with fermented bean paste and vegetables.
Beef Chow Fun (干炒牛河): A Cantonese specialty of wide, flat rice noodles stir-fried over high heat ("wok hei") with tender beef, bean sprouts, and dark soy sauce.
Chow Mein (炒面): Stir-fried wheat noodles tossed with vegetables and proteins like chicken, beef, or shrimp, coated in a savory soy-based sauce.
Egg Drop Soup (蛋花汤): A comforting, simple soup made with whisked eggs in a savory chicken or vegetable broth.
Regional Specialties Hot Pot (火锅): A communal dining experience where guests cook raw meats, seafood, vegetables, and noodles in a simmering pot of broth (often fiery Sichuan mala broth) right at the table.
Char Siu (叉烧): Cantonese-style barbecue pork, marinated in a sweet and savory sauce, roasted on a spit, and often served over rice or in noodle bowls.
Moo Goo Gai Pan (蘑菇鸡片): A lighter Cantonese stir-fry made with sliced chicken, mushrooms, and various crisp vegetables in a mild, savory broth.
Sweet and Sour Pork (糖醋排骨): Crispy pieces of pork tenderloin coated in a bright, sweet, and tangy sauce, frequently served with bell peppers and pineapple.
Wonton Soup (云吞汤): A Cantonese soup featuring delicate, thin-skinned dumplings filled with minced pork and shrimp, served in a clear, flavorful broth.
